What each layer predicts

How to read this
  • Each column is one piece of your prompt. The model reads text in pieces called tokens, so long words are split, for example "Eiffel" into "Eiff" and "el".
  • Each row is one processing step (layer) of the model. The bottom row is the input, the top row is the model's final answer.
  • A cell shows the word that this layer would predict next if the model stopped there. A stronger blue means more confident.
  • The amber line marks where the model settles: from there up, every layer agrees with the final answer.
  • Some models have an extra MTP block , shown as the row above the final layer. It guesses two tokens ahead, one position earlier than the layers, but its guess is drawn in the same column as theirs, so every cell in a column guesses the same next token. The first cell is empty.
  • The dashed draft columns go past your prompt. The MTP block guesses on from its own guesses, as in speculative decoding: under each column is the token it reads, the first one being the model's final prediction, and the MTP cell is its guess for the token after. Only the MTP row has cells there.
  • Shading by KL compares the whole list of guesses, not just the top word: a stronger blue means the layer already thinks like the final answer.
  • The small i markers explain terms; click or tap them.
  • Hover or tap a cell for its top 5 guesses. Clicking or tapping a cell also follows one word through all layers.
